## 2026-11-15 · Summary
Hou, He, Cen, Liu, Dong, Kharlamov and Tang (WWW 2023). GraphMAE2 keeps the masking of GraphMAE, which hides the
features of a random share of the nodes and rebuilds them, and changes only the decoding. The authors argue that
masked feature reconstruction depends on how discriminative the input features are and suffers when they are noisy, so
they add two regularisers: multi-view random re-mask decoding (the encoded embeddings are re-masked at random several
times, and every decoding must still recover the input features) and latent
representation prediction (the masked nodes' embeddings must also match the representation a target-generator network
makes from the unmasked graph). For graphs with hundreds of millions of nodes it also builds dense subgraphs by local
clustering before masking. Tested on large datasets (ogbn-Arxiv, ogbn-Products, MAG-Scholar-F, ogbn-Papers100M) and on
the small Cora, Citeseer and PubMed.

Results
- Abstract: "at least 2.45% improvements over state-of-the-art baselines on ogbn-Papers100M with 111M nodes and 1.6B
  edges".
- Linear probing (Table 3): better than all self-supervised baselines on all datasets; over GraphMAE, +1.91% on
  MAG-Scholar-F and +2.35% on Papers100M (absolute).
- Fine-tuning with 1% and 5% of labels (Table 4): above 5.1% gain on ogbn-Products with the pre-trained model, and 80.52%
  with 5% of the data against GraphSAINT 80.27% and Cluster-GAT 79.23% with 100%. On Papers100M only GraphMAE2 and
  GraphMAE beat the random-initialised model.
- Ablation (Table 6): removing latent representation prediction costs -1.58% on ogbn-Products and -1.91% on
  ogbn-Papers100M, removing the multi-view random re-mask -0.45% and -0.73%. Removing the feature reconstruction and
  keeping only latent prediction collapses the representation.
- Small datasets (Table 5): it outperforms all baselines, but the gain over GraphMAE is not as large; the authors think
  it is because bag-of-words features are less noisy as targets.
- Masking rate, re-masking rate: 0.5 and 0.5, with 3 re-masks, for all four large datasets (Table 10).

For the thesis
- Same family as graphmae-2022 and the same question for masking-training. What it adds is that it keeps the masking and
  changes the decoder, so the thesis can treat "what to hide" and "what fills it" as two separate choices.
- The latent target is an idea for a Revit graph: predict the embedding of a hidden element, not its raw attributes
  (which may be exact numbers or text). It is a design choice to test, not a result on buildings.
- It uses a fixed 0.5 mask rate for all four large datasets and does not study the rate; for that, graphmae-2022 and
  li-2023 are the references.
- It is a representation learner on citation, product and academic graphs; no generation and no measure of new against
  copied. The paper says the gain is smaller where input features are clean, which may or may not be the case for Revit
  parameters.
