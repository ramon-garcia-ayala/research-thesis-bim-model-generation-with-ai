## 2026-11-15 · Summary
Sun, Zhou, Zhou, Li and Li (ECCV 2024). Forest2Seq generates 3D indoor scenes object by object with an autoregressive
transformer, and its claim is that the order in which the objects are given to the transformer matters. The unordered set
of furniture is first grouped: DBSCAN clusters the objects on a distance that mixes the distance between box centres with
1 minus the generalized IoU, the largest object of each cluster becomes the root of a tree, siblings are shuffled, and the
tree is linearized by breadth-first search (BFS). Flexible pieces that fit several clusters, such as cabinets, are tied
to each possible parent, which gives a forest; in training one tree of the forest is picked at random. The model takes a
floor-plan mask as start token and predicts the next object (class, size, position, orientation) from the previous ones;
the "masked" in its section title is the causal attention of the decoder, not a masking task. It is trained and tested
on 3D-FRONT (around 10k professional scenes: bedrooms, living rooms, dining rooms, libraries) and compared with FastSynth,
SceneFormer, ATISS, COFS, LayoutGPT and DiffuScene.

Results
- Order ablation on living rooms (Table 4; KL, FID: lower is better; CAS: closer to 50% is better). Random single order:
  KL 20.0, FID 49.4, CAS 83.7%. Fixed order: 17.9, 49.8, 80.1%. Tree + BFS: 7.90, 36.1, 68.1%. Random multiple (ATISS
  style, shuffled each time): 13.1, 43.3, 76.4%. Forest + DFS: 9.40, 40.5, 71.7%. Forest + BFS: 5.90, 35.2, 68.0%.
- Inconsistency of the set of sequences (average Hamming distance between two sequences of one scene): 9.54 for random
  multiple, 4.01 for forest + DFS, 1.87 for forest + BFS. The authors read the better BFS result as a result of this
  greater consistency. Qualitatively (Fig. 5), main furniture is placed less accurately with DFS.
- Forest statistics (Table 1): 11.7 nodes and 2.83 trees per living room, 10.9 and 2.51 per dining room, 5.00 and 1.27 per
  bedroom, 4.61 and 1.14 per library.
- Against the state of the art (Table 2) the text says it surpasses all baselines in KL divergence and has the most
  favorable FID and CAS; the table cells did not extract in a form that can be matched to the rows, so no figure is
  quoted from it. User study (Table 3a, 37 participants, 51 rooms per type): preferred in 43.3%
  of bedrooms, 52.0% living rooms, 58.0% dining rooms and 48.5% libraries.
- Parameters 9.99 (Table 3b, labelled MB) against 36.1 for ATISS, 19.4 for COFS and 74.1 for DiffuScene; inference
  0.160 s against 0.204, 0.129 and 34.9 s.

For the thesis
- The nearest published comparison to "one element, then its neighbours" against other orders, in a placement task of
  objects with classes, boxes and a floor-plan constraint (placement-order-experiments). Order from a tree walked
  breadth-first beats a fixed order, a random one and DFS on the same model.
- Its parent-child tree comes from clustering by distance, not from host or contained-in relations. A Revit graph already
  has such relations, and walking them breadth-first would be the thesis's version of the same idea. That is an
  inference; the paper does not use BIM.
- Only one-at-a-time orders are compared. Placing everything at once and refining (the other side of
  placement-order-experiments) is not in it, so MaskGIT is the reference for that side.
- The ablation is on one room type (living rooms) with one model, and the data are furniture in rooms from 3D-FRONT.
  The numbers say that order matters there, not how much it will matter for building elements.
- It is also the closest example of assemble-node-by-node with an autoregressive transformer outside of IFC: objects are
  placed one by one, each conditioned on the ones before.
