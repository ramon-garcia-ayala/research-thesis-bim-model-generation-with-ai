## 2026-11-15 · Summary
Bu, Mehrab and Karpatne (Virginia Tech, arXiv preprint 2305.15562, under review). The paper is about the order in
which an autoregressive model generates the parts of a graph. It looks at paired graph generation, where each input
(an image, desired properties) has exactly one target graph, and argues that the order has been chosen by habit (BFS or
DFS) without proof. It treats ordering as dimensionality reduction: map every graph token to one number and sort by it.
From that it proposes "latent sort", an autoencoder with a 1-D bottleneck trained with a latent gradient penalty, and
a backbone, GraphART (a modified GPT-2 with no GNN, tokens are edges joined to their two nodes' features). Orders
compared: mean squared sort, lexicographical sort, SVD low-rank sort, BFS, DFS and latent sort. Four datasets: Toulouse
Road Network, a synthetic Planar Graph set (averaged over 10 random runs), OpenPSG scene graphs and a circuit graph set
of 347k graphs with 3 to 6 resonators.

Results
- Toulouse Road Network (Table 1, SMD, lower is better), GraphART: latent sort 0.0075, mean squared sort 0.0081,
  lexicographical 0.0422, SVD 0.0751, BFS 0.0893, DFS 0.0761. For comparison, GGT 0.0158 and GraphRNN 0.0245.
- Planar graphs (Table 2, GraphART, EMD): latent sort 0.0038, mean squared sort 0.0031, lexicographical 0.0141, SVD
  0.0381, BFS 0.0217, DFS 0.0213. The paper says BFS and DFS "consistently exhibit inferior performance on both
  datasets", and thinks they do fine when a distribution is wanted but badly when an exact target graph is required.
- Scene graphs (Table 3): lexicographical sort is best, followed closely by latent sort; mean squared sort cannot be
  used because the one-hot tokens all have the same value, and BFS and DFS can hardly be applied since the graphs are
  mostly small and disconnected.
- Circuit graphs (Table 4): GraphART and GraphLSTM beat Circuit-GNN; latent sort has a slight advantage.
- The authors note that a simple order that is "easy to learn" (mean squared sort) does very well, and that latent sort
  does not always beat a heuristic made for the task. Experiments are limited to small graphs.

For the thesis
- It supports the claim in placement-order-experiments that the order is a variable and not a detail: the same model on
  the same data gives errors of 0.0075 or 0.0893 depending on the order (Table 1). The authors say that, to the best of
  their knowledge, no earlier work had compared orders in this way.
- It is the case of "one by one" only: all orders are sequences for an autoregressive model. It has no "all at once"
  and no "one and then its neighbours" in the sense of the thesis, although BFS and DFS are close to the neighbours
  case, and there BFS and DFS come out worst.
- That BFS loses here goes against Forest2Seq (sun-2024), where BFS beats DFS for scenes. The difference is the task:
  the metric here wants an exact match to one target graph, not a plausible graph.
- No buildings, no BIM, graphs of roads, scenes and circuits. No check of new against copied; the metrics measure
  distance to the single target graph, which for a thesis that wants "new" is the opposite of what is asked.
- It is a preprint marked under review, so cite it with that caveat.
