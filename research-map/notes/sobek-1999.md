## 2026-10-23 · Summary
Set-based concurrent engineering (Toyota), popularised against the point-based practice of U.S. carmakers. Three
principles: map the design space; integrate by intersection; establish feasibility before commitment. Teams keep a set
of acceptable alternatives and winnow it after more vetting, instead of freezing one design early.

Trade-offs: looks inefficient (slower iterations, critical parameters chosen later), but studies report fewer costly
iterations, better quality and shorter time to market. Ford & Sobek model it as real options: keeping options open has
a calculable pay-off. Little guidance exists on how to implement it.

For the thesis: one-to-ten is point-based; many-to-five is set-based. Modular slack and regeneration are two answers
to future change.
