## 2026-11-08 · Summary
A language (PMESS, written in XML) to cut a partial IFC model out of a complete IFC model. The user writes a Selection Set:
the object types, attributes and relations wanted. Seven rules then decide what else must come along so that the result is
still a valid IFC file. Three basic rules: keep the one IfcProject and what it references; keep every entity that the target
entity references (its Essential Set); keep the entities in its inverse attributes, stopping at shared basics such as
IfcOwnerHistory, IfcCartesianPoint and IfcLocalPlacement (the Particular Set). Three extraction rules (by type, by relation,
by attribute rule) and one rule that drops everything not required. It is coded in C++ with IfcPlusPlus and tested on one
ArchiCAD shopping mall: 8 floors, 148,564 m², about 101 M, 1,862,673 IFC instances.

Results
- Table 1, size of the partial file and share of the original: slabs (5284) 19.9 M, 19.7%; doors (911) 19.2 M, 19.0%;
  transport elements (64) 15.7 M, 15.5%; beams and columns (6886) 13.8 M, 13.7%, with 173,763 instances (9.3%).
- By relation, the underground floors B1 and B2: 32.1 M, with 942 columns, 1380 beams, 1253 walls, 351 doors, 69 slabs and
  148 stairs.
- By rule, curtain walls by placement: 2.37 M and 0.59 M.
- Semantic validity: the IFC File Analyzer shows that the extracted objects are those requested.
- Syntactic validity: the partial models were imported back into ArchiCAD.

For the thesis
- The only paper here that says what a part cut from a real IFC must contain to remain a valid IFC file. The closure rules
  (references forward and inverse, relation entities pulling both ends, a stop list of shared entities) are what a
  decomposition step needs if its parts are to be valid on their own.
- Parts here are chosen by a person, by type, relation or rule. It does not find parts, learn anything, or put parts together.
- Not shown: any use for generation, how to merge two partial models, or an IFC schema validator run on the output.
- One model, one authoring tool. The text says models from MagiCAD, Revit and Tekla were also used to check feasibility,
  but no result is given.

Doubts from reading it (Claude's, not the paper's)
- Validity is claimed from a count of objects and from one re-import into ArchiCAD. The text says the files could be imported
  into Revit and Tekla, but only ArchiCAD is shown. The seven rules are argued, not tested against a checker.
- A part pulls a lot with it: 911 doors give 309,332 instances (16.6%), more than the 292,912 of the 5284 slabs. How much
  of that is geometry and how much is related entities is not said.
