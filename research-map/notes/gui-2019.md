## 2026-11-08 · Summary
A way to cut a light "partial model" out of a central IFC model for one stakeholder, for a distributed design platform.
The partial model view lists the central model's GlobalID, the GlobalIDs of the chosen IfcObjects and the properties
wanted. An algorithm walks the IFC instances and keeps only those properties, stored directly on each object with no
relation entities. When needed, the IFC partial model can be rebuilt from the central one through the GlobalIDs (the authors
call this IFC-traceability). It runs inside BIMserver on three open IFC projects (Hospital 6.26 MB, Office_MEP 40.9 MB,
Conference Center 68.7 MB), against the central model and an IFC-validated partial model made with Won et al. (2013).

Results
- First-floor view, Hospital: 1,209 major entities in the central model, 49 in both partial models; relation entities 5,054,
  1,277 and 0; property entities 785, 218 and 0.
- Transmission against the central model: 29.5%, 52.3% and 37.3%, as the text gives them. Against the IFC-validated model:
  78.9%, 74.4% and 71.3%. Loading and rendering is "about 25% less" than the IFC-validated model.
- Parsing is slower than for the IFC-validated model: 36.1 s against 35.6, 117.8 against 100.3, 219.5 against 216.3.
- Asset-management view without geometry (Table 2): 45.35, 3763.4 and 1021.6 KB, which is 1.92%, 4.79% and 3.82% of the
  IFC-validated model.
- Validity: the partial model is not IFC-valid. It is checked by comparing object and property ids with the IFC-validated
  model (same set), and by the BIMserver renderer logging no missing-object errors.

For the thesis
- A concrete pattern for a light part with traceability by GlobalID: the part keeps what a task needs and a key back to the
  whole. That is the shape a decomposed part needs if it is to be put back into a model.
- No learning, no generation. The view is "arbitrarily defined" by hand (the authors' words). No IFC or Revit is written.
- The part is not IFC-valid, and nothing checks a merged result. Lack of write-back is listed as a limitation; the authors
  say part of that work "will appear in another paper".
- Three open projects, one floor each, no repeated runs or spread reported.
- Complements Deng et al. (2020): they keep the part valid as IFC, this one gives up validity to be light.

Doubts from reading it (Claude's, not the paper's)
- Three percentages in the text do not match its tables. Hospital against IFC-validated: 1817.1 / 2359.9 = 77.0%, text
  78.9%. Conference Center against central: 19,062.6 / 50,349.5 = 37.9%, text 37.3%. Hospital loading in the asset view:
  0.43 / 1.17 = 36.8%, text 26.5% (which is the storage time, 0.31 / 1.17).
- The asset properties (originalValue, owner, user) were inserted by hand with arbitrary values. The comparison measures
  size, not the data of a real project.
- The PDF is a submission draft. In its response letter a reviewer says Revit can change an object's GlobalID after an
  edit; the authors answer that their Revit plugin builds the id from project name, version and internal id. GlobalID alone
  is not a stable key. This is not in the paper body.
