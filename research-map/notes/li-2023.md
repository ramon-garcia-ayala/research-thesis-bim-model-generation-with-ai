## 2026-11-15 · Summary
Li, Wu, Sun, Chen, Tian, Zhu, Meng, Zheng and Wang (KDD 2023). MaskGAE is a masked graph autoencoder whose task is
masked graph modeling (MGM): it hides a share of the edges, encodes the graph that stays visible, and predicts the
hidden edges (the structure decoder) and the degree of the nodes they touch (the degree decoder). Two ways of masking
are used: edge-wise (each edge hidden with probability p) and path-wise (root nodes are sampled with probability q and
random walks from them are hidden). The authors argue that graph autoencoders are in essence contrastive learners that
maximise the mutual information between paired subgraph views of an edge, and that masking reduces the redundancy of
those two views. Tested on eight benchmarks (Cora, CiteSeer, Pubmed, Photo, Computer, arXiv, MAG and Collab) for link
prediction and node classification.

Results
- Mask ratio (Fig. 4): performance improves smoothly as a large share is masked, and the best is reached at a critical
  value, "e.g., 0.7", of p or q; values that are too large or too small give poor results. The default is p = 0.7 for
  edges, and q = 0.7 for paths (70% of the nodes taken as roots).
- Link prediction (Table 3): both variants lead on all metrics (AUC, AP, Hits@k) against the compared algorithms, and
  beat the vanilla GAE on the three citation datasets by 5% on average in AUC and AP. It sets a new state of the art on
  the large Collab dataset.
- Node classification (Table 4): both variants outperform or match the best contrastive approaches across all
  datasets, and MaskGAE outperforms GraphMAE on six out of seven datasets.
- Embedding size (Fig. 6): sizes from 64 to 512 perform about equally; 64 is enough in most cases.
- Encoder (Table 5): GCN is the best of GCN, GAT and SAGE for both masking strategies.
- Appendix C: GraphMAE reconstructs features and MaskGAE reconstructs structure (edges and degrees).

For the thesis
- It is the structure side of masking-training: hide relations (edges) instead of attributes, which is the case
  where the model has to learn which elements are connected. For a Revit graph, that is hiding host, adjacency or
  containment links and asking the model to put them back. The paper also gives a reason for high ratios, which is that
  graphs are redundant.
- For deconstruct-for-training it gives another cut of one model into examples: not only removing nodes but hiding
  edges or paths of the same graph, so one model yields many training examples.
- The path masking hides adjacent edges together, which is closer to hiding "a wall and what it hosts" than hiding single
  random edges; the paper does not test that on buildings.
- Its tasks are link prediction and classification of existing nodes, on citation, co-purchase and collaboration
  networks. It predicts whether an edge exists; it does not create nodes or give them attributes, and nothing is
  generated. No measure of new against copied.
- The best 0.7 is for these graphs; the paper does not say the value carries to other graphs, and GTGAN++
  (tang-2024) finds 40% for room graphs.
