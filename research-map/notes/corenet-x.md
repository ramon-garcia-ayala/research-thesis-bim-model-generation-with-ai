## 2026-10-18 · Summary
History
- 1995: Singapore launches CORENET for online permits.
- 2000s: e-PlanCheck, the first automated code checker on IFC models, on FORNAX (novaCITYNETS, private), which
  adds code-checking semantics to IFC objects. Piloted in Norway and New York; never became a standard.
  Criticism (ITcon 2016): a black box; experts could not see or change the rules, only the vendor could.
- 2018 started, soft-launched 18 Dec 2023: CORENET X. One coordinated BIM model in IFC-SG instead of a set of
  plans per agency; agencies review it together and give one response. Gateways: Design → Construction →
  Completion. BIM mandatory for new projects ≥ 5,000 m² new GFA (URA DC23-07).

Why it is hard (AECbytes 2005): "Handrails are not required for any staircase having not more than 5 risers."
The model often holds the stair as one volume; the checker has to infer risers, landing and handrail.
The rule is easy; the hard part is that the model carries the information the rule needs.

Legal weight: the Model Checker is a supplementary pre-submission self-check. Today only its MVP is live: IFC
and Code of Practice quality checks; regulatory rules come in phases, starting with geometric and spatial ones.
The Qualified Person still coordinates and signs; agency reviewers decide.

Unconfirmed: consultancy guides say CORENET X is mandatory for all new projects from Oct 2026; why e-PlanCheck
was dropped.

Sources: ura.gov.sg circular DC23-07; www1.bca.gov.sg CORENET X technological enablers;
