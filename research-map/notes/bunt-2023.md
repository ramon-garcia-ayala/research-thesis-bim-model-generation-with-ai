## 2026-10-23 · Summary
Architecture and engineering graduate students used an optimization tool, recorded with eye-tracking and the screen.
Three kinds of iteration: by the designer alone, by the optimizer alone, and by the designer using the optimizer's
feedback. By how often and when these happened, students made partial, crude or complete optimization cycles.

All could run the optimizer; not all brought its results into their decisions. Explaining the algorithm and the tool is
not enough; they must be taught inside a design approach.

For the thesis: the closest prior study. GFit's difference: practitioners, real projects, manual tools first.
To read: Bunt's dissertation (Penn State, advised by Nathan Brown) — its fourth study extends the coding to
practitioners and compares by cognitive load; it marks where GFit's study must differ.
