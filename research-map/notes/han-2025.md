## 2026-11-08 · Summary
BIGNet pretrains a graph neural network (GraphMAE2 with 2-layer GAT encoder and decoder) on graphs taken from Revit models
through the Revit API: 176 graphs, 974,991 nodes and 3,414,280 edges, from hospital, residential and industrial buildings,
one graph per floor or per region. Components, and also their spatial and topological relations, become nodes with
feature vectors (shape, size, family names, position, distances, angles). Half of the nodes are masked and the network
reconstructs them. The encoder is then reused to find injected errors in 16 other real Revit models.

Results
- Local spatial range, homogeneous graph, Average F1: 0.78 (0.2 m), 0.87 (0.3 m), 0.81, 0.80, 0.77 (0.6 m). 0.3 m wins.
- Transfer with 30% of the data (homogeneous): feature extraction + GAT 0.87, + MLP 0.77, fine-tuning 0.68. No pretraining:
  0.51 (MLP) and 0.50 (GAT). The paper reports gains of 59.5% and 72.7% over the non-pretrained models.
- Homogeneous graphs beat heterogeneous ones in the main setting (0.87 against 0.64 at 0.3 m); the heterogeneous ones train
  about five times slower. Their F1 rises with more labels (0.44 at 10% to 0.83 at 50%).
- Errors in the 16 models are artificial: 30% of wall types, door heights and MEP-fitting links were changed and labelled.
  Counts (train / validation / test): topological 1174 / 1138 / 1350, semantic 81 / 77 / 94, data range 40 / 43 / 46.

For the thesis
- The largest corpus taken through the Revit API so far in the map, and a working recipe: relations as nodes, so one node
  classifier covers elements and links. It fits the author's Revit data lakes better than the IFC route of Hornung et al.
- Masked pretraining is one way to learn parts and neighbours without labels. But it reconstructs node features inside an
  existing graph. It does not generate a new model, and it writes no Revit or IFC element.
- Validity is only error detection on injected errors, not a check that a built model is valid. Errors are artificial.
- The authors propose to combine learned checks with rules read from standards ("precise recognition" plus "comprehensive
  coverage"): a point for explicit-vs-learned.

Doubts from reading it (Claude's, not the paper's)
- The paper does not say whether the 16 test models are also among the pretraining models, nor whether the split is by project
  (it says five regions train, five validation, six test, which equals 16).
- Reading the bars of Figure 18 (0.77 against 0.51, 0.87 against 0.50), a relative gain would be about 51% and 74%, not 59.5%
  and 72.7%. The paper does not say how the percentages are computed or whether they average several runs.
- The abstract says 3.5 million edges; Section 3.1.5 says 3,414,280.
