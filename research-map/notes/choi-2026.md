## 2026-11-15 · Summary
Choi, Park and Kim (Seoul National University, arXiv 2601.07218v2, "under review at TMLR", not yet published). SceneNAT
generates 3D indoor scenes (a list of furniture objects with class, appearance and layout) from a text instruction, with
a masked non-autoregressive transformer. A scene is a matrix with one row per object and one column per attribute, all
discretised (layout attributes in 64 bins). Training is masked modeling at two levels: token level (single attributes of
an object) and object level (a whole object), with a BERT-style replace-and-remask policy and a cosine mask schedule.
Generation starts from a fully masked scene matrix and fills it in parallel steps in the way of MaskGIT: each step
re-predicts the tokens with low confidence (maximum softmax probability), with the remasking ratio set by a cosine
schedule. A relational reasoning module, trained with (subject, predicate, object) triplets and used at inference only
through its features, takes the spatial relations of the instruction. Experiments are on the extended 3D-FRONT dataset
(with instruction annotations from InstructScene), for three room types, against the autoregressive ATISS and the
diffusion-based DiffuScene and InstructScene, with LayoutGPT as an LLM reference; results are the mean and standard
deviation over 10 independent trials.

Results
- Table 1: SceneNAT has the best iRecall (instruction adherence) and visual quality (FID, FID-CLIP) in all three room
  types, with competitive physical plausibility and fewer object collisions. ATISS, the autoregressive one, is plausible
  on some accessibility metrics but has no control by language.
- Speed (Table 3, Fig. 6a): good quality in 30 steps, against 1,000 for DiffuScene and 110 (100+10) for InstructScene.
  The paper says it is about 5x faster than InstructScene and over 24.7x faster than DiffuScene, with over 20% fewer
  parameters than InstructScene and 4.3x lower TFLOPs; the timing is at batch size 128 over 50 iterations.
- Masking ablation (Table 4): replace-and-remask is essential and removing it severely degrades quality. Token-level and
  object-level masking both improve FID; object-level masking matters more for iRecall; both together are best.
- Granularity (Table 5): 32 to 256 bins make little difference; coarser bins slightly lower iRecall; 64 is used.
- Robustness (Fig. 5, Fig. 7): it generalises to unseen wording and rewritten prompts although it is trained on
  templated instructions, and it is evaluated with 5 and 6 relational constraints, more than the maximum of 4 seen in
  training (Fig. 5).

For the thesis
- It is the closest case to the three-way comparison of placement-order-experiments, inside one paper: ATISS (one by
  one, autoregressive) against SceneNAT (all at once, filled in about 30 to 50 parallel steps) on the same data and task,
  with quality and time reported. It does not include "one and then its neighbours" and its quality measure is about
  fit to a text instruction.
- For masking-training it masks both single attributes and whole objects in the same model, and says that the
  whole-object level is the one that helps instruction adherence most. For a Revit model that is the cut between
  hiding a parameter of an element and hiding the element.
- It is a scene of furniture, with a small fixed set of attributes per object and a maximum number of object slots, not
  a BIM model with hosted elements and levels, and the relation between objects enter only as text constraints.
- It checks collisions (physical plausibility), a rough analogue of validity, and does not check whether a scene is
  new or copied from 3D-FRONT.
- It is a preprint under review, so cite it as such.
