## 2026-11-15 · Summary
Tang, Shao, Sebe and Van Gool (IEEE TPAMI 2024). GTGAN++ is a graph Transformer GAN that generates house layouts from a
room graph (the bubble diagram), plus house roofs and commercial building layouts. Its added step is a self-guided
pre-training by masking: nodes and edges are hidden together at a high ratio and rebuilt by an asymmetric graph
autoencoder, in the style of MAE. A node branch masks nodes, an edge branch masks edges of the complementary graph, and
the loss is the cross-entropy on the masked nodes and edges only. After pre-training only the encoder is kept and the
generator is fine-tuned on complete graphs. House layouts use LIFULL HOME plans (realism by user rating, FID, and graph
edit distance for compatibility); roofs use the RoofGAN CAD dataset; buildings use the 120,000 volumetric designs of
BuildingGAN.

Results
- Masking ratio from 10% to 90% on house layouts: 40% is the best, and above it the remaining nodes and edges do not hold
  enough information to rebuild the graph and the learned embeddings get worse (Fig. 13, FID and compatibility are both
  lowest at 40%).
- Training time: GTGAN takes about 52 hours; GTGAN++ takes 24 hours plus 8 hours of pre-training, about 32 hours in total.
  HouseGAN takes 40 hours and HouseGAN++ 46 hours.
- Ablation on the "10-12" subset (Table 4, as described in the text): either masking strategy alone already surpasses the
  model without masking, and node plus edge masking together is better than each alone.
- Roof generation, 3 primitives: FID 7.5 and RMMD 4.2 for GTGAN++, against 9.3 and 5.5 for GTGAN and 11.1 and 7.5 for
  RoofGAN. With 4 primitives: 7.1 and 5.6 against 9.6 and 7.2 and 13.8 and 10.9 (Table 2).
- Building layout: FID 0.0528 and connectivity accuracy 0.617 for GTGAN++, against 0.0732 and 0.581 for GTGAN and 0.0845
  and 0.569 for BuildingGAN (Table 3).
- Table 1 (house layouts, five subsets) did not come through in the extracted text; the paper states that the method
  beats all other methods on all metrics there.

For the thesis
- The clearest number for the training step in architecture: how much to hide is not "as much as possible". Here the best
  is 40%, lower than the 0.7 to 0.9 that GraphMAE reports for some generic graphs, and the paper's reason is that above
  40% too little of the graph is left to rebuild it. A Revit graph repeats walls, doors and windows more than a room
  graph does, so it could tolerate a higher ratio; that is an inference, not a claim of the paper, and something the
  thesis can measure (masking-training).
- The masking ratio is a parameter to sweep from 10% to 90% on the thesis graphs, with the curve of Fig. 13 as the
  model of how to report it.
- The graph is rooms, not building elements, and nothing in it is written back to a model; the output is a layout image.
  As a comparison point for assembling back (assemble-node-by-node) it shows what a generator from a room graph achieves,
  not what a Revit assembly needs.
- The limitation the authors state applies to the thesis too: the datasets do not capture the distribution of real
  architectural layouts, and the method is sensitive to the style of the graph representation.
- To keep from memorizing, the house-layout experiment trains without the samples of the subset it generates; the same
  idea as holding a model out (new-not-replicated).
