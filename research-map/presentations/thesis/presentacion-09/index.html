1 · Introduction · Abstract09

Decomposing BIM models into rooms to generate new ones.

Real Revit models are taken apart into rooms and their relations; an AI learns by masking them; a validator checks what it proposes.

G. Ramón García Ayala · MaCAD, IAAC · midterm

My thesis asks how to turn real Revit models into training data. I take a model apart into rooms and their relations, an AI learns by hiding parts and predicting them, and a validator checks what it proposes. I will go through it like a paper: problem, question, references, method, results.

2 · Research question09

Can BIM models be decomposed into rooms and their relations to generate new, valid models?

Why the room: it is the smallest unit where a plan’s logic shows. Understand the unit first, then scale: room → plan → building.

So the question: can BIM models be decomposed into rooms and their relations to generate new, valid models? The room is the smallest unit where a plan's logic shows: what it holds, what it touches, how you enter. The idea is to understand the unit first, then scale to the plan and the building.

2 · Research question · How much to predict09

How much to predict: the room.

  1. Buildingout of scope
  2. Floor planstretch

Given its boundary, predict what a room holds and how it connects.

How much should the AI predict? Think of a pyramid. One element is done; the relation, which wall hosts a door, is partly done. The target is the room: given its boundary, predict what it holds and how it connects. The floor plan is the stretch goal; the whole building is out of scope.

2 · Research question · New and valid09

What new and valid mean.

New

How far from training

  1. another storeytoday: close to a copy
  2. another buildingof a typology seen
  3. a new typologynever seen
Valid

Two layers of checks

  1. proposal
  2. hard rules · script
  3. judgement · LLM via MCP

A room on each side of every door; does a bathroom opening onto the kitchen make sense?

The drop in the scores, from near to far, is how far the prediction reaches.

Two words in the question need a measure. New is distance from training: another storey, another building, a typology never seen; the drop in the scores tells how far it reaches. Valid has two layers: hard rules by script, like a room on each side of every door, and a soft judgement by an LLM through MCP.

4 · Method · Tokens09

A token is one word the network reads; an element, a short sentence of them.

A door 7 tokens · today

  1. CLS:doorwhat it is
  2. TYPE:M_Single-Flush | 0915 x 2134mmits Revit type
  3. LVL:01 - Entry Levelits level
  4. X:45where
  5. Y:14where
  6. SILL:0sill height
  7. HOST:wallwhat holds it

A room 6 tokens today · its relations next

  1. CLS:roomwhat it is
  2. TYPE:Room | Menits name
  3. LVL:02 - Floorits level
  4. X:44where
  5. Y:7where
  6. AREA:15how big
  7. BOUNDS:wallsits walls
  8. ADJ:roomsrooms beside
  9. ACCESS:roomsthrough a door
  1. One token per attribute: what it is, where, how big, what it joins.
  2. Position in 0.5 m cells from the model’s corner: X:45 is 22.5 to 23 m in.
  3. 403 different tokens in the whole model: the vocabulary the network knows.
  4. Next, in blue: the room’s relations become tokens too.
  1. pipeline/out/experiments/e1-tokens-bfs/tokens.jsonl, Nov 2026 · the door and the room of E1’s piece

How does a network read a model? Each element becomes a short sentence of tokens: what it is, its type, level, position, what holds it. A door is 7 tokens. A room is 6 today; next, its relations become tokens too: the walls that bound it, the rooms beside it, and access through a door.

4 · Method · Masking09

Hide a part: the network guesses it from its neighbours.

  1. 1Hideone door of a piece of 20; only its level stays
  2. 2Lookthe transformer reads every visible neighbour, 3 rounds
  3. 3Answerdoor or window? · which type? · which wall? · where on it?
  4. 4Scoreagainst the most frequent answer and the nearest copy
  5. →Next: hide moreone element → the openings of a room → its walls → a storey; split by building
  • W wall
  • F floor
  • D door
  • R room
  1. pipeline/experiments/e2_mask_openings.py, Nov 2026 · drawn on the real piece of E1

This is masking. Hide one door of a piece; the transformer reads every neighbour, three rounds, and answers: door or window, which type, which wall, where. Each answer is scored against two baselines. Next, the hidden part grows: the openings of a room, its walls, a whole storey.
