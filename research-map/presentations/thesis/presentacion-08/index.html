Abstract

Decomposing BIM models into rooms to generate new ones.

Revit models taken apart into rooms and relations; an AI learns by masking them; a validator checks what it proposes.

G. Ramón García Ayala · MaCAD, IAAC · midterm

My thesis asks how to turn real Revit models into training data. I take a model apart into rooms and their relations, an AI learns by hiding parts and predicting them, and a validator checks what it proposes. I will go through it like a paper: problem, question, references, method, results.

Problem

Real BIM models are not ready to teach an AI.

  1. Noardo et al., 2021, An Inspection of IFC Models from Practice: for each use case, 5 to 10 of 57 IFC models usable as they are
  2. Geç & Güzelci, 2026, ML-based decision support for BIM component specification: Macro-F1, door leaf material

Offices have years of models, but they are not ready to teach an AI. Noardo found only 5 to 10 of 57 usable as they are. And what a model learns on one project drops on the next: from 0.926 to 0.209 once a third typology comes in. The data exists; its shape is the problem.

Research question

Can BIM models be decomposed into rooms and their relations to generate new, valid models?

Why the room

A door alone means little; it means something because it joins two rooms.

the 91 rooms of the sample

So the question: can BIM models be decomposed into rooms and their relations to generate new, valid models? The room is the first level where architectural logic shows: access, light, adjacency. A door alone means little; it matters because it joins two rooms. Behind me, the 91 rooms of the sample.

How much to predict: the room.

  1. Buildingout of scope
  2. Floor planstretch
  3. Roomtarget
  4. Relationpartly · E2
  5. Elementdone · E2

How much should the AI predict? Think of a pyramid. One element is done; the relation, which wall hosts a door, is partly done. The target is the room: given its boundary, predict what it holds and how it connects. The floor plan is the stretch goal; the whole building is out of scope.

What new and valid mean.

01New

How far from training: another storey, another building, an unseen typology.

02Valid

Hard rules by script; a soft judgement by an LLM through MCP.

Two words in the question need a measure. New is distance from training: another storey, another building, a typology never seen; the drop in the scores tells how far it reaches. Valid has two layers: hard rules by script, like a room on each side of every door, and a soft judgement by an LLM through MCP.

Reference 1 · Tang 2024

Room graphs, learned by masking.

Take: the room graph; the ratio as a parameter. Gap: it ends in an image, not a model.

  1. Tang, Shao, Sebe & Van Gool, 2024, Graph Transformer GANs with Graph Masked Modeling for Architectural Layout Generation, IEEE TPAMI: masking ratio from 10% to 90%, best at 40% (Fig. 13)

The first key reference is Tang 2024. It generates layouts from a room graph, pre-trained by hiding nodes and edges. They swept how much to hide, from 10 to 90 percent: the best was 40 percent; above it, too little is left to rebuild. I take the room graph and that sweep; but it ends in an image.

Put side by side: Tang learns rooms but ends in an image. Liang reaches Revit, but from synthetic plans. This thesis takes the corner neither covers: real BIM models from practice, decomposed into rooms, and a result that is a Revit model again, checked by a validator.

Method

Six steps, one round trip.

  1. 01ExtractRevit → JSON
  2. 02Graphtokens, relations
  3. 03Maskhide a part
  4. 04Predicta transformer
  5. 05Placeback in Revit
  6. 06Validaterules + MCP

The method is six steps. Extract the Revit model to JSON; write it as a graph of tokens and relations; mask a part; a small transformer predicts it; a script places the answer back in Revit. The first five run today. The sixth, validate, is where the MCP server now goes.

Training

Hide more, step by step.

  1. One element · today
  2. the openings of a room
  3. the walls of a room
  4. a storey’s partitions

Against most frequent and nearest copy; split by building, not by storey.

the plan · illustrative

Training climbs the pyramid: the hidden part grows from one element, to the openings of a room, its walls, and a storey's partitions. Each step is scored against two baselines, the most frequent answer and the nearest copy. And the split changes: by building, not by storey.
